What the lawsuit alleges

"Designed for privacy." That's what owners were told.

According to a class action filed in federal court in San Francisco in March 2026, Meta marketed its AI glasses as private and in the user's control while footage from those glasses was allegedly being handled very differently.

01

Recordings sent to Meta

Video and audio captured through the glasses, including footage from inside people's homes, was allegedly transmitted to Meta's servers.

02

Reviewed by humans overseas

The footage was allegedly passed to a third-party contractor, where human workers in Kenya viewed, catalogued, and labeled it.

03

Used to train AI

Those labeled recordings were allegedly used to train Meta's artificial intelligence models, without owners' knowledge or meaningful consent.

"No reasonable consumer would understand 'designed for privacy, controlled by you' and similar promises like 'built for your privacy' to mean that deeply personal footage from inside their homes would be viewed and catalogued by human workers overseas."
From the complaint in Bartone, et al. v. Meta Platforms Inc., et al., No. 3:26-cv-01897 (N.D. Cal.)
