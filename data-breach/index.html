Why it matters

Companies that collect your data have a duty to protect it.

Businesses, hospitals, banks, and service providers ask for your most sensitive information. When they don't use reasonable security, or wait too long to tell you it was stolen, the law may hold them accountable.

01

Your data was stolen

Hackers or unauthorized parties accessed personal information a company stored about you, such as your SSN, financial accounts, or health records.

02

The risk doesn't expire

Stolen Social Security numbers and dates of birth can't simply be changed. They can be sold and misused for identity theft years later.

03

You may be compensated

Data breach claims may seek reimbursement for out-of-pocket losses, lost time, credit monitoring, and other harm caused by the breach.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does it cost anything to register?

No. Registering and your case review are free. Cases like this are typically handled on a contingency basis, which means you pay no attorney's fees unless there is a recovery.

I haven't had any fraud yet. Can I still sign up?

Yes. Many people affected by a breach haven't seen fraud yet. The exposure of your information, and the time and cost of protecting yourself, may still matter.

I lost or threw away my breach notice letter. Is that a problem?

No. You can still register. If you remember which company sent it, that's enough to start. If you find the letter or email later, please keep it.

I already signed up for the free credit monitoring the company offered. Does that affect my rights?

Generally, enrolling in credit monitoring offered after a breach doesn't waive your right to pursue a claim, but terms vary. We can review any paperwork with you during your case review.

What should I do right now to protect myself?

Consider freezing your credit with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, reviewing your bank and card statements, and watching for unfamiliar accounts. You can report identity theft at IdentityTheft.gov. Be cautious of anyone contacting you about the breach and asking for your SSN or passwords.

Is there a deadline?

Legal claims are subject to time limits, and some data breach cases move quickly. Registering early helps make sure your rights are protected.
